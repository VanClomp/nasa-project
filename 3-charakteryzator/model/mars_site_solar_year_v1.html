A Mars year of sunlight at the landing sites

Solar energy reaching flat ground each sol, for every site where a lander has operated and two candidates. Orbit geometry is exact; dust follows a representative seasonal pattern, not measurements.

Dust year

Surface energy through the year

Watt-hours per square metre per sol on a horizontal surface. Pick up to six sites. Sol 0 is the northern spring equinox.

Dust in the air

Optical depth used by the model at the reference elevation (−1.4 km). Lower sites sit under more dust, higher sites under less.

Year-round ranking

Sorted by the worst sol of the year, which is what a solar-powered mission has to survive.

What is computed

  • Sun distance and season from Mars's orbit: eccentricity 0.0934, axial tilt 25.19°, perihelion at Ls 251°, 668.6 sols per year.
  • Day length and sun height from latitude, integrated across each sol.
  • Dust dimming with a two-stream scattering model, so scattered light still counts.
  • Mean pressure from elevation alone, assuming 610 Pa at the datum and an 11.1 km scale height.

What is assumed

  • The dust curve is a typical shape, the same at every longitude and latitude. The storm scenario peaks near Ls 195 at optical depth 5.
  • Flat ground, no hills on the horizon, no dust settling on panels, no clouds or frost.
  • Coordinates, elevations and temperature ranges are approximate values from memory, to be replaced with archive data.
  • Arcadia Planitia is one representative point, not a selected site.
